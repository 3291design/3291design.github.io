× Definition of Terms

Gear Inches

A traditional measure representing the equivalent diameter of a direct-drive wheel. Higher numbers = harder resistance.

Formula: (Chainring ÷ Cog) × Tyre Diameter

Cadence / Speed

Calculates your ground speed based on your pedaling rate (revolutions per minute). Efficiency is 80–95 RPM.

Speed: (GI × π × Cadence × 60) ÷ 63,360

Cross-Chaining

Highlighted values represent extreme chain angles (Big-to-Big or Small-to-Small). Values are faded and italicized to indicate these gears are mechanically inefficient, cause excessive wear and should be avoided.

Drivetrain Capacity

The total 'chain slack' managed by the rear derailleur. Critical for determining the required rear derailleur cage length.

Formula: (Big Ring - Small Ring) + (Big Cog - Small Cog)

Total Gear Range

The span between your easiest climbing gear and hardest sprint gear. A higher % means a wider range of gears and more versatility.

Formula: (Highest Gear Ratio ÷ Lowest Gear Ratio) × 100%

× How the Calculator Works

The Compatibility Guard Engine

The Compatibility Guard analyzes your selected chainrings and cassette cogs in real-time. It calculates total wrap capacity to flag setups that exceed standard rear derailleur limits, and warns against extreme ratios like oversized gravel cogs paired with racing doubles.

Chainwrap & Derailleur Capacity

Capacity measures the physical chain slack your rear mech must accommodate. If a setup exceeds 47T of total capacity, a long-cage (SGS) or wide-clutch derailleur is typically required to keep the chain from sagging in small-small combinations.

Cross-Chaining Mechanics

The engine automatically highlights severe chain angles (big-ring-to-big-cog or small-ring-to-small-cog). These combinations cause lateral friction, premature chain wear, and power loss, which is why they are visually muted in the output chart.
