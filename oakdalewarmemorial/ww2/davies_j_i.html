John Ivor Davies was born around 1915 in South Wales, the son of Albert Edward and Mary Davies. He grew up in Rhiw-syr-Dafydd, Oakdale, and worked as a general labourer before the outbreak of the war.

He left his labouring job behind to join the Pioneer Corps, a vital branch of the British Army tasked with a wide array of heavy logistics, light engineering, asset protection, and structural recovery tasks. Amidst the growing uncertainty of the conflict, John married Ivy Ellen Davies in late 1940.
By early 1941, Private Davies was stationed with his Pioneer Corps unit in the North East Surrey sector. During this critical stage of the home front mobilisation, Pioneer units in the Greater London and Surrey areas were working at a gruelling pace. They were heavily involved in clearing debris, stabilising bombed buildings, and constructing essential defence and logistical structures to maintain Britain's home front operations.
It was while serving under these high-pressure conditions that Pte. Davies lost his life on 19 March 1941.
Following his death, Private Davies' body was returned to his loved ones in Wales. He was laid to rest in the peaceful grounds of Penmaen Congregational Chapelyard near Oakdale, Wales, where his grave is meticulously cared for by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission (view listing: 2736338). His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
