The UK Home Front
Not confined to overseas, dangerous domestic duties, bomb disposal, air defense, and training accidents deeply impacted wartime Oakdale.
While major overseas campaigns dominated public consciousness, the United Kingdom itself became a heavily militarised fortress and an active theatre of risk. Personnel stationed domestically faced hazardous duties ranging from high-risk bomb disposal and coastal defense to intensive military training exercises under wartime blackouts.[1]

In this second part, we honor the eight servicemen from Oakdale who lost their lives on British soil in direct defense of the mainland between 1940 and 1943 before completing overseas campaign star requirements.
1939 – 1943 Defense of the Realm
The constant threat of enemy bombing raids during the Blitz, combined with the urgent necessity of neutralizing unexploded ordnances, placed enormous pressure on home-service personnel. As bombs fell on British cities, specialized bomb disposal companies of the Royal Engineers worked in subterranean craters where a single slip carried fatal consequences.[2]
ATS personnel manning anti-aircraft defences, 1942
Photo credit: National Army Museum
Simultaneously, Royal Artillery anti-aircraft batteries manned searchlights and heavy guns at night, while RAF night-fighter squadrons flew interception patrols through the night skies to defend coastal ports and industrial hubs.[4][5]
Beyond frontline air defense, mobilizing millions of troops required a massive domestic infrastructure. Servicemen stationed across naval depots (such as HMS President III), Pioneer Corps construction detachments, and RAF flight schools faced severe physical demands.[3] Flight training crashes, naval instruction hazards, heavy construction accidents, and sudden illness under strained wartime housing conditions exacted a steady toll on home soil.[3]
Clearing bomb-damaged houses, London, 1940
Photo credit: National Army Museum
Between 1940 and 1943, every one of these eight local men made the supreme sacrifice on British soil while safeguarding the home front.[1] Though their service time preceded standard thresholds for overseas campaign stars, their daily devotion ensured the domestic machinery of war endured through the nation's most vulnerable hours. Brought home to rest in local chapelyards, their memory remains permanently etched into valley history.
The vital defense of the home front is remembered through the sacrifice of Oakdale servicemen who died on active service in the United Kingdom, including Lemuel George Daniels, Gwyn Davies, John Ivor Davies, William Ward Davies, Vernon Ellway, Alfred Richard Lewis, Charles Shipman, and Iorwerth Walter Watkins.
