John Henry Morris Jenkins was born to John and Sarah Jessie Jenkins, and baptised at the Penmaen Independent Chapel near Oakdale and Blackwood, Wales. Before the outbreak of global conflict, John’s daily life was defined by hard manual labor as he worked as a colliery worker in the South Wales coalfield. In 1940, he married Alice Mary Jenkins in Buckinghamshire.

John entered the British Army holding the rank of Private. He was posted to the 2nd Battalion, Sherwood Foresters (Notts and Derby Regiment), a distinguished regular army unit serving within the 3rd Infantry Brigade of the British 1st Infantry Division.
By the time Private Jenkins joined his battalion in the line, the unit was highly experienced, having survived the Dunkirk evacuation, the Tunisia Campaign in North Africa, and the brutal amphibious landings at the Anzio beachhead. By late 1944, they were spearheading the slow, grinding push against the heavily fortified German Gothic Line in Northern Italy.
By late November and early December 1944, the 1st Infantry Division was operating under immense hardship. The Allied advance had reached the approach to the city of Faenza, positioned along the vital Route 9 highway. Conditions were horrific, with freezing temperatures, torrential winter rain, and deep mud bogging down armour and supply lines. The infantrymen of the 2nd Sherwood Foresters were tasked with clearing dug-in German paratroopers and panzer grenadiers from fortified farmhouses and waterlogged river obstacles along the Lamone River. The fighting was characterised by close-quarters night attacks and relentless enemy artillery shelling.
It was during these brutal operations to secure the approaches to Faenza that Private John Jenkins was killed in action on 6 December 1944, at 27 years old. Initially buried near where he fell, his remains were later moved to the Faenza War Cemetery in Italy, an area containing over 1,100 Commonwealth casualties of the late 1944 winter battles (view listing: 5340978). His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
