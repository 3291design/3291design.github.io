Albert Thomas was born to Gomer and Elizabeth Ann Thomas. He later married Irene Thomas, and the couple resided in the village of Argoed, near Blackwood and Oakdale, Wales.

Albert entered military service by enlisting in his local regional regiment, the South Wales Borderers. In July 1944, while stationed in the hard-fought Normandy theater, Private Thomas was officially transferred into the Black Watch (Royal Highlanders).
He was subsequently posted to the 1st Battalion, The Tyneside Scottish, a historic unit that operated formally within the Black Watch brigade structure. As a front-line infantryman, Albert participated in the grueling Allied advance through France, Belgium, and into Germany, enduring the immense hardships of close-quarters European combat.
Following the formal surrender of Nazi Germany, Albert survived the war but passed away on 8 January 1946 at the age of 28, while still on active military service during the post-war demobilisation period.
Albert Thomas' body was returned home to his grieving family and wife in South Wales, and he was laid to rest in Penmaen Congregational Chapelyard near Oakdale, where his grave is meticulously maintained by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission (view listing: 3911230). His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
