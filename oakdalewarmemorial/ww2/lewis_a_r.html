Albert Richard Lewis was born in South Wales, the son of Richard and Rosina Lewis. Prior to the outbreak of the Second World War, he lived in the mining community of Oakdale and worked underground as a coal trimmer, contributing to the vital industrial fuel supply of industrial Britain.
Leaving his civilian life behind, Lewis enlisted in the British Army and was posted to the 1st Battalion, The Welch Regiment. As part of the regular army, the 1st Welch Regiment saw intensive service in the Mediterranean and Middle East theatres, playing a key role in the North African Campaign against Axis forces.
By May 1943, the bitter fighting in North Africa was drawing to a close following the surrender of Axis forces in Tunisia. However, the physical toll of prolonged desert warfare, disease, and arduous active service continued to claim the lives of soldiers even after major combat operations ceased. Private Lewis passed away on active service on 16 May 1943 at the age of 27.
Following his death, Private Lewis's body was returned to his home valleys in South Wales. He was laid to rest in the peaceful grounds of Penmaen Congregational Chapelyard near Oakdale, where his grave is meticulously cared for by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission (view listing: 3972238). His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
