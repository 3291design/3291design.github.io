Charles Shipman was the son of Henry and Annie Shipman. He went on to marry Winifred Shipman, and the couple made their home in Pontllanfraith, Monmouthshire, in the South Wales Valleys.

Charles chose a career at sea well before the Second World War erupted, entering the Senior Service and earning the official service number P/K59636. The "P" prefix within his service designation specifies that his home port and mobilisation base was the historic Portsmouth Division. He specialised in the engineering branch as a Stoker, tasked with the operation of shipboard propulsion systems, boilers, and heavy machinery, eventually progressing to the senior non-commissioned rate of Petty Officer Stoker.
By 1941, Petty Officer Stoker Shipman was officially on the books of HMS Victory II. In Royal Navy administrative terms, Victory II was a prominent Naval Shore Establishment located at Crystal Palace/Sydenham (working alongside accounting sections at Portsmouth). This vital depot acted as the primary training and accounting command hub for specialised personnel of the Royal Naval Division and engineering drafts. Senior Petty Officers stationed here were essential for processing, training, and dispatching personnel to active fighting fleets worldwide.
While fulfilling his wartime responsibilities, Petty Officer Stoker Shipman passed away on 20 September 1941. Following his death, his body was returned home to his grieving wife and family in Monmouthshire. He was laid to rest in Jerusalem Chapelyard, Woodfieldside. His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
