William James Charles was a Welsh soldier from Oakdale who served in the 2nd Battalion, The Welch Regiment during the Second World War. Prior to deploying overseas to the Far East theater, his family home was located at 72 Markham Crescent, Oakdale, where he lived with his parents, David and Annie Charles.

Lance Corporal Charles fought with General Slim’s famous Fourteenth Army—often referred to as the "Forgotten Army"—during the brutal, hard-fought campaign to liberate Burma from Japanese occupation. Operating as part of the 62nd Indian Infantry Brigade attached to the 19th Infantry Division, the 2nd Battalion operated in dense, sweltering jungle terrain, enduring torrential monsoon rains, malaria, and severe logistical challenges.
By the spring of 1945, the Allied Fourteenth Army had successfully pushed south, breaking Japanese lines at Imphal, Kohima, and Mandalay. Following the liberation of Rangoon in early May, remaining Imperial Japanese forces attempted to break out eastward through the dense jungles towards the hills.
In mid-May 1945, the 2nd Battalion was stationed around Toungoo, a strategically vital town on the main road and railway axis linking Mandalay and Rangoon. During intense mop-up operations in the surrounding jungle, Lance Corporal Charles’ unit encountered stubborn enemy resistance. On 17 May 1945, he was struck down in combat, falling in the line of duty just as the campaign was nearing its victorious end—and only 90 days before Victory over Japan (VJ) Day.
Because he fell in the remote jungles of Burma, Lance Corporal Charles is commemorated at the Rangoon War Cemetery in Myanmar (Burma), maintained by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission (view listing: 3909382). His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
