Gwyn Davies was born on 20 March 1910 in South Wales, the son of Samuel and Mary Ann Davies. Raised in the village of Pontllanfraith, he later relocated to Somerset, where he established a successful civilian career. Settling in the coastal resort town of Burnham-on-Sea, he ran a local bakery and confectionery business and built a life with his wife, Gwyneth Mary Davies.

He enlisted in the Royal Air Force Volunteer Reserve and trained for the highly demanding role of an Observer. In the early years of World War II, the Observer served as the navigator and tactical core of a light-to-medium bomber crew, responsible for guiding the aircraft accurately to a target and ensuring its payload hit the mark.
By late 1940, Sergeant Davies was assigned to No. 17 Operational Training Unit (OTU), formed at RAF Upwood in Cambridgeshire. The unit's primary mission was to train newly minted crews on the twin-engine Bristol Blenheim light bomber, preparing them for the intense realities of frontline operations with Bomber Command.
The air war in 1940 demanded rapid, rigorous training, often conducted in poor winter weather and congested British airspace. On 26 November 1940, Sergeant Davies boarded a Bristol Blenheim bomber for a training exercise. During the flight, the aircraft encountered catastrophic trouble, plummeted out of control, and crashed near Kettering, Northamptonshire. Sergeant Davies died alongside his fellow crew members in the wreckage at the age of 30, a stark reminder of the immense dangers faced by RAF crews even before deploying to the battlefields over occupied Europe.
Following the tragedy, Sergeant Davies’ body was returned home to the valleys of South Wales to be buried close to his birthplace. He was laid to rest in Jerusalem Chapelyard, Woodfieldside, Wales, where his grave is meticulously cared for by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission (view listing: 903408). His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
