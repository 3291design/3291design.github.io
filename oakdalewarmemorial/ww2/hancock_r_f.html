Raymond F. Hancock was born in the industrial town of Tredegar, Monmouthshire, and later relocated to live in the neighbouring town of Blackwood. He was part of a generation of young men growing up in the South Wales Valleys during the interwar years.

Raymond entered military service holding the rank of Gunner, joining the 90th Light Anti-Aircraft Regiment, Royal Artillery, specifically serving within 312 Battery. His regiment had a proud local lineage: it was originally formed in November 1941 by converting the infantrymen of the 7th Battalion, South Wales Borderers into anti-aircraft gunners. Raymond and his comrades were trained to operate Bofors guns, highly effective weapons designed to track and shoot down low-flying enemy dive-bombers.
In March 1943, Raymond’s regiment deployed overseas to North Africa, later serving in Pantelleria before landing on mainland Italy in December 1943. In January 1944, 312 Battery took part in Operation Shingle, landing on the beaches of Anzio as part of the British 1st Division with the goal of outflanking the German winter defensive lines and opening the road to Rome.
The Allied forces quickly became pinned down in a notoriously flat, open beachhead surrounded by German forces holding the high ground. By mid-February 1944, the Germans launched a massive, desperate counter-offensive, known as Operation Fischfang, to push the Allies back into the sea. The front line was chaotic, and anti-aircraft units were frequently forced to drop their sights and fire directly into oncoming German infantry.
Gunner Raymond Hancock was killed in action on 17 February 1944, during the heaviest days of this defensive stand. He was one of 17 men in his regiment lost during that bloody month. He was laid to rest alongside thousands of his brothers-in-arms in the Anzio War Cemetery in Italy, cared for by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission (view listing: 4077520). His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
