Albert Stewart Parry was born around 1910 in Monmouthshire, Wales. He was the son of Robert and Dorothy Parry, residing in the community of Oakdale, Blackwood, Monmouthshire.

Albert joined the Royal Navy and attained the rank of Able Seaman, a role requiring experienced seamanship, helmsman skills, and the capability to man the ship's weaponry. He was stationed aboard HMS Jupiter, a modern J-class destroyer commissioned on the eve of the war in June 1939. Jupiter saw intensive wartime action, including Mediterranean deployments, before being transferred to the Far East to reinforce the American-British-Dutch-Australian Eastern Striking Force in early 1942.
In late February 1942, HMS Jupiter and her crew were deployed to stop a massive Imperial Japanese Navy invasion fleet heading toward Java. On 27 February 1942, during the chaotic night phase of the Battle of the Java Sea, Jupiter was steaming near the northern coast of Java. At approximately 21:16, a violent explosion ripped through the ship. While initially feared to be an enemy torpedo, subsequent historical records verified that Jupiter had struck a defensive naval mine laid earlier that day by the Dutch minelayer Gouden Leeuw.
The explosion proved fatal to the vessel, and despite the crew's best efforts, the destroyer foundered and sank. Able Seaman Albert Stewart Parry, aged 32, was among the casualties who perished when the ship went down.
Parry’s final resting place lies in the waters off the coast of Indonesia. He is officially commemorated with honour by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission on the Plymouth Naval Memorial (view listing: D/SSX 19839). His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
