Iorwerth Walter Watkins was born to William and Sarah Jane Watkins, growing up in the South Wales Valleys where his family resided in Pontllanfraith, Monmouthshire. He joined the Royal Air Force, demonstrating the high technical and mathematical aptitudes required to qualify for navigation and radar interception work.

Upon earning his observer's wings and promoting to Sergeant, Iorwerth was posted to No. 29 Squadron RAF. By late 1940, as the intense daylight dogfights of the Battle of Britain gave way to the nocturnal bombing campaign of the Blitz, No. 29 Squadron became a pioneering unit tasked with developing night-fighting tactics from RAF Digby and its satellite airfield at RAF Wellingore.
The squadron operated the Bristol Blenheim Mk IF, a light bomber heavily modified into a long-range night fighter. Crucially, these aircraft were fitted with the world's first Airborne Interception (AI) radar arrays. As the observer, Sergeant Watkins bore the intense workload of navigating in pitch darkness while interpreting crude radar displays to guide his pilot toward unseen enemy targets—a dangerous mission with low-level training conducted in treacherous winter conditions.
On Thursday, 19 December 1940, Sergeant Watkins and his three crewmates boarded Blenheim Mk IF (Serial Number L6612) at RAF Wellingore for a low-level AI radar practice flight bound for RAF Digby. While conducting exercises near Leadenham, Lincolnshire, the aircraft encountered a sudden, severe downdraught along the edge of a high escarpment. Flying low with virtually no safety margin, the Blenheim struck treetops at the corner of a small wood and crashed violently, killing all four crew members instantly.
Following the tragedy, Iorwerth’s body was brought home to his grieving parents in South Wales. He was laid to rest with military honors in Jerusalem Chapelyard, Woodfieldside, under the care of the Commonwealth War Graves Commission (view listing: 979229). His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
