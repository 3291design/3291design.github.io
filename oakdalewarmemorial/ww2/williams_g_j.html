Gwilym James Williams was born in early 1920 to Gwilym Henry and Gladys Louisa Williams. He grew up in the model village of Oakdale, near Blackwood in Monmouthshire, South Wales.

Gwilym joined the Welsh Guards and was posted to the 3rd Battalion, a unit officially raised in October 1941 to expand the regiment’s wartime strength. He underwent rigorous combat training, learning to operate as part of a highly disciplined shock-infantry unit before deploying overseas.
The 3rd Battalion saw major combat during the Tunisian Campaign in North Africa before re-routing to the European mainland. In February 1944, Gwilym and his brother guardsmen landed at Naples, Italy, stepping straight into the grinding, waterlogged winter conditions of the Italian Campaign. By the spring of 1944, the battalion was thrown into the heart of the Allied assault against the Gustav Line—Germany's deeply fortified mountain defensive network across central Italy targeting Monte Cassino.
Combat at Cassino was notoriously horrific, defined by uphill assaults, devastating mortar fire, collapsing rocky terrain, and brutal close-quarters fighting. Though the historic monastery at the peak finally fell in mid-May, retreating German rear-guards continued launching fierce counter-attacks. It was during these intense follow-up operations to clear the path northwards that Guardsman Williams was killed in action on 28 May 1944 at the age of 24, just as the road to Rome was breaking open.
Following his death, Gwilym was buried near the battlefields where he fell. Today, he lies in a permanent resting place within the Cassino War Cemetery in Italy, meticulously cared for by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission (view listing: 2734976). His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
