Wyndham Royce Silcocks was born in late 1920 to Leonard and Violet May Silcocks and grew up in Oakdale, South Wales. Coming of age just as Europe plunged into the Second World War, Wyndham stepped forward to volunteer for hazardous flying duties within the Royal Air Force Volunteer Reserve (RAFVR).

Upon securing his crew qualifications and the rank of Sergeant, Wyndham was posted to No. 179 Squadron RAF, a highly specialised maritime patrol and anti-submarine warfare unit operating under RAF Coastal Command. The squadron flew twin-engined Vickers Wellington medium bombers heavily modified for maritime duties, often equipped with advanced ASV radar arrays and powerful "Leigh Lights"—massive, high-intensity searchlights used to visually illuminate and strike surfaced German U-boats under cover of darkness. The squadron’s Latin motto, Delentem deleo ("I destroy the destroyer"), perfectly reflected their dangerous hunter-killer mission parameters.
By late 1943, No. 179 Squadron was heavily active in the Mediterranean, supporting the aftermath of Operation Torch and safeguarding Allied naval convoys transiting Atlantic choke points. To sustain these frontline operations, replacement aircraft and fresh crews endured treacherous ferry flight routes running directly from the United Kingdom to the fortress island of Gibraltar. Crews flew completely blacked-out over thousands of miles of open ocean, navigating severe weather, technical failures, and the constant threat of interception by long-range enemy aircraft.
On 19 November 1943, Sergeant Silcocks and his fellow crew members departed the UK on a transit leg bound for Gibraltar. Somewhere along this vast oceanic corridor, their Wellington bomber went down. No wreckage was ever recovered, and the entire crew was posted as missing, presumed killed in action.
Because he was lost at sea with no known grave, Sergeant Wyndham Silcocks is permanently commemorated on the Malta Memorial in Valletta, Malta (view listing: 1320730). The monument stands as a lasting tribute to thousands of Commonwealth airmen lost across Mediterranean and Atlantic waters. His supreme sacrifice is permanently commemorated on the Oakdale War Memorial.
