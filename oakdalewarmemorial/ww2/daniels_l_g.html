Lemuel George Daniels

Lemuel George Daniels was born on 26 May 1914, the son of Henry and Mary Jane Daniels. He grew up in the Sirhowy Valley of South Wales, a region dominated by coal mining. In 1938, Lemuel married Mary Elizabeth Beacham, embarking on family life just as Europe stood on the brink of war. According to the 1939 National Register, the young couple lived at 1 Sir Ivor’s Road, Pontllanfraith, where Lemuel worked as a colliery miner below ground.

Despite being in a reserved occupation that initially exempted him from military conscription to maintain Britain's energy supplies, Lemuel stepped forward to serve his country at sea. Upon entering the Royal Navy, Able Seaman Daniels was assigned to HMS President III, a specialized shore establishment responsible for training and assigning naval personnel to defensively equipped merchant ships (DEMS).
These specialized DEMS gun crews served as the crucial line of defense protecting vital civilian merchant convoys from enemy U-boat and aerial attack. Able Seaman Daniels was deployed aboard the merchant vessel SS Medee, operating in British coastal waters. In early April 1942, his maritime duties brought him back to South Wales when the Medee docked at the port of Cardiff.
On 6 April 1942, tragedy struck through the inherent hazards of naval service. While on duty aboard ship in Cardiff Docks, Lemuel suffered a severe accidental fall. The traumatic injuries sustained proved fatal, and he passed away that same day, joining the ranks of thousands of servicemen who gave their lives in line-of-duty accidents while keeping Britain's supply routes open.
Following his death, Lemuel’s body was returned to his grieving family in the Sirhowy Valley. He was laid to rest with military honors in Jerusalem Chapelyard, Woodfieldside, Wales, where his grave is meticulously cared for by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission (view listing: D/JX 311941). His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
