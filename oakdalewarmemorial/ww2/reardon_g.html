Glyndwr Reardon was born to Mr and Mrs Alfred Reardon and grew up in Oakdale, South Wales. Volunteering for aircrew duties in the Royal Air Force Volunteer Reserve as a 19-year-old, he qualified as a wireless operator and front gunner and was posted to 15 Squadron RAF.

On the night of 10–11 February 1941, Sergeant Reardon took off from RAF Wyton as part of a six-man crew aboard a Vickers Wellington IC bomber (serial number T2702 'LS-H'). They were participating in a major 222-aircraft bombing raid targeting Hannover, Germany—a historic operation that also marked the very first operational deployment of Britain's Short Stirling heavy bombers.
After successfully completing their bombing run, the crew was intercepted on their return journey over the Netherlands by a German Luftwaffe Messerschmitt Bf 110 night fighter piloted by Hauptmann Walter Ehle. Severe damage forced the pilot to attempt an emergency night landing, crash-landing the aircraft onto the frozen, ice-covered surface of lake IJsselmeer near Kampen.
Sergeant Reardon was the sole casualty of the crash. Positioned in the nose of the aircraft, he became trapped when the heavy forward section broke through the ice, causing him to drown. The remaining five crew members escaped the sinking fuselage, however, they were soon captured by German forces, and spent the remainder of the war as Prisoners of War (POWs).
Sergeant Reardon's body was later recovered and laid to rest in the Jonkerbos War Cemetery in Nijmegen, Gelderland, Netherlands, where his grave is meticulously registered and cared for by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission (view listing: 967118). His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
