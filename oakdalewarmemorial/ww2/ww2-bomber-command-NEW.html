Bomber Command & Operational Aircrews
Flying through night skies over occupied Europe, Oakdale’s airmen operated at the tip of the spear in the war's most perilous campaign.
When the Second World War engulfed Europe, a generation of young men from the mining valleys of South Wales stepped away from civilian life to answer the call of the skies. Joining the Royal Air Force and Royal Air Force Volunteer Reserve (RAFVR), these recruits trained intensively as pilots, navigators, bomb aimers, wireless operators, flight engineers, and air gunners before being posted to frontline operational squadrons. Under RAF Bomber and Coastal Commands, they qualified for the 1939–1945 Star with Bomber Command Clasp immediately upon engaging in active operational combat sorties.[1]

In this first part, we honor the demanding operational realities, night-time combat risks, and supreme sacrifices borne by Oakdale’s aircrew.
