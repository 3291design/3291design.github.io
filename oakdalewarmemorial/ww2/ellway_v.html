Vernon Ellway was born on 22 October 1918 to Howard John Ellway and Florence Ellway. He grew up in the tight-knit community of Blackwood, Monmouthshire, where his family ran a local business. According to the 1921 Census, the family resided at 135 High Street, Blackwood. His father, Howard, was an independent hairdresser working on his "own account," establishing a family trade that Vernon would eventually follow.

By the time the 1939 Register was compiled on the eve of the war, Vernon was 20 years old and working alongside his father as a hairdresser’s assistant on High Street.
In September 1939, just as Britain entered the Second World War, Vernon stepped forward to serve his country. He enlisted in the Royal Air Force Volunteer Reserve (RAFVR) and was sent to RAF Padgate in Lancashire, a major recruitment and basic training hub for the RAF. The Volunteer Reserve consisted of civilian enlistees who formed the backbone of the RAF's rapid wartime expansion.
While stationed on active duty, Aircraftman 2nd Class Ellway was killed on 30 October 1941, just eight days after his 23rd birthday.
Following the tragedy, Aircraftman Vernon Ellway's body was returned home to his grieving family. He was laid to rest in Jerusalem Chapelyard, Woodfieldside, where his grave is meticulously cared for by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission (view listing: 1031941). His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
