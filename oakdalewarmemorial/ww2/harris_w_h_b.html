William Herbert Beddow Harris was a naval communications specialist who served his country during the critical maritime struggles of 1944. Born around 1922 to Francis John and Gertrude May Harris, he grew up in the local area before eventually settling in the town of Blackwood, Monmouthshire.

As a young man during the war, he met and married Myra Rennis Harris. The young couple established their home in Blackwood, though their time together was cut short by the demands of global conflict.
William entered the maritime war when called up for the Royal Navy, operating as an Ordinary Telegraphist. The "D" prefix in his service number indicates he was deployed out of the Plymouth Port Division (HMS Drake). Telegraphists were highly trained technical personnel responsible for transmitting and receiving coded wireless messages, maintaining the fleet's lifeline under hazardous conditions.
Ordinary Telegraphist Harris gave his life at sea or while on active naval station duties on 17 February 1944, at the age of 22. Coincidentally, his passing occurred on the exact same day that fellow Blackwood resident Gunner Raymond Hancock was killed in action at the Anzio beachhead in Italy.
Following his death, his body was returned to his grieving family in South Wales. He was laid to rest in Jerusalem Chapelyard, Woodfieldside, where his physical headstone remains today under the care of the Commonwealth War Graves Commission (view listing: D/JX609151). His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
