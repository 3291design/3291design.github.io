Frederick Moses Cyril Henry Harris was born to Moses and Mabel Harris. He grew up in the village of Argoed, located in the Monmouthshire valleys near Blackwood and Oakdale, South Wales.

Upon earning his air gunner wings and promoting to Sergeant, Frederick was assigned to No. 148 Squadron of the Royal Air Force Volunteer Reserve. Operating out of Brindisi in southern Italy, No. 148 Squadron was repurposed as a specialized unit working hand-in-hand with the Special Operations Executive (SOE). Flying heavily modified Handley Page Halifax heavy bombers, these "Special Duties" crews flew completely blacked-out at night through narrow mountain passes at low altitudes to drop weapons, ammunition, agents, and supplies to underground resistance groups fighting Nazi occupation.
On the night of 11 February 1944, Sergeant Harris and his seven crewmates boarded Halifax Mk.V (Serial Number JN959) for a secret mission designated SOE Operation Stables 6, tasked with dropping covert supplies to resistance forces in Albania. After successfully crossing the Adriatic Sea and dropping their payload over the designated partisan drop zone, the crew encountered severe cloud cover and volatile winter weather on the return leg over Yugoslavia.
While navigating the mountainous terrain in pitch darkness, the Halifax collided with a mountain peak near Cetinje, Montenegro, crashing violently near the village of Bjelosi. Of the eight men on board, seven were killed instantly. The sole survivor, air gunner Sergeant A.H. Elkes, was thrown clear of the wreckage, captured by German forces, and spent the remainder of the war as a Prisoner of War.
Following the war, the remains of the fallen crew were recovered from the mountain and reinterred in a centralised military resting place. Today, Sergeant Frederick Harris lies buried alongside his crew mates in the Belgrade War Cemetery in Serbia, meticulously registered and cared for by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission (view listing: 1836450). His name remains proudly honoured within the local history of Oakdale, keeping his memory alive for the generations that follow on the Oakdale War Memorial.
